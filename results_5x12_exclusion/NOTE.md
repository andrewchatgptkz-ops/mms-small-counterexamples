# Theorem (computer-assisted): every 5-agent, 12-good instance with at most two valuation types has an MMS allocation

GPT-6 Pro, 2026-09-05 (prompts P16-v2 S2 and S2b); reductions in `THEOREM.md`; re-checked by Claude.

**Statement.** Let 5 agents with additive valuations share at most two distinct valuation functions over 12 indivisible goods. Then an allocation giving every agent at least her maximin share exists. Consequently the two-type version of f(5) equals 12 (the accepted 5×13 two-type instance has no MMS allocation), and a 5-agent, 12-good instance without an MMS allocation — if one exists — must use at least three valuation types. f(5) ∈ {11, 12} stays open.

**Proof structure.** (0) One type or the 1+4 split: the odd agent takes her favourite bundle of the common type's MMS partition. (1) Normalise: divide each type's values by its MMS, then shrink values inside each witness bundle proportionally so every witness bundle is worth exactly 1 (values stay positive; an allocation meeting 1 in the normalised instance meets the original MMS). Rows = the minority type's witness bundles (2 agents, "R"), columns = the majority type's (3 agents, "C"); goods = edges of a 5×5 bipartite multigraph. (2) Two goods in the same row and column merge into one; the witness partitions survive with unit sums, so both MMS values stay 1; Hummel's n+6 theorem gives an allocation for the 11-good instance, which lifts back. Hence a counterexample has a simple grid. (3) A singleton R row {g}: give g to an R agent; the remaining 1+3 instance on 11 goods has 4-agent MMS ≥ 1 for both types and is all-but-one-identical, so (0) applies. (4) Two singleton C columns {g}, {h}: at least three R rows avoid both, their C-total is ≤ 3, the two lightest sum to ≤ 2; give them to the R agents, g and h to two C agents, the rest (≥ 1) to the third. (5) What remains: simple grids with all rows of degree ≥ 2 and either all columns of degree ≥ 2 (462 oriented classes up to row/column permutations: 37 simple connected, 2 simple disconnected, 192 multi connected, 231 multi disconnected — the multi ones are already covered by (2) but were proved anyway) or exactly one column of degree 1 (72 classes). Each class is excluded either by a value-independent allocation (two whole rows + three whole columns, disjoint) or by a complete allocation-branching proof: 50 hard-row/hard-column roots, LP relaxation v_g ≥ δ, unit witness sums, v(S) + δ ≤ 1 for assumed-losing sets, exact rational Farkas duals at the leaves, branching on explicit disjoint allocations, downward closure, symmetry reuse. Totals: 534 classes, 341 direct, 193 proved, 220 145 nodes, 3 862 exact dual leaves.

**Positive vs non-negative values (Claude).** The proof objects assume positive values. If a counterexample with zero values existed, adding a small ε to every zero (finitely many allocations and partitions; MMS and every utility move by at most 12ε) would keep it a counterexample with positive rational values and two types — contradiction. So the theorem holds for non-negative valuations too.

**Checking.** `verify.py` (producer's, standard library only, ~25 s) enumerates both classes itself and validates every proof; `check_5x12_proofs_claude.py proofs.json.gz` is Claude's independent re-implementation (same 220 145 nodes, 3 862 leaves). Claude also re-enumerated the classes independently (642 600 row multisets → 34 450 with all degrees ≥ 2 → 462 classes with the same 37/2/192/231 split; 72 one-singleton classes) and checked the reductions (0)–(4) by hand.
